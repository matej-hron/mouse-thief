1932 · Western Australia

The War Australia Lost to Birds

In 1932 about 20,000 emus moved into the wheat farms of Western Australia, eating crops and trampling them. Many of the farmers were ex-soldiers, and they asked the government for machine guns.

The Defence Minister, Sir George Pearce, sent Major G. P. W. Meredith of the Royal Australian Artillery with two soldiers, two Lewis guns and 10,000 rounds of ammunition. Rain delayed the start, and the operation began on 2 November 1932.

It went badly. The emus split into small groups and ran out of range. In one ambush a gun jammed after only about a dozen birds had been shot. A gun mounted on a truck could not aim on the rough ground, and the truck could not keep up with the birds. By 8 November about 2,500 rounds had been fired, and estimates of the birds killed ranged from 50 to 500. The soldiers were called back.

Round two

Farmers asked for help again, and Meredith returned on 13 November. When the operation ended on 10 December he reported 986 emus killed, with almost 10,000 rounds used, about ten bullets per bird. He compared the emus to tanks because they kept going after being hit.

The emu population barely changed, and the episode became known as the Emu War: the war Australia lost to birds.
