1822 · London, Central America, Paris

The Man Who Invented a Country

Gregor MacGregor was a Scottish soldier who had fought in the South American wars of independence. Back in Britain in 1821, he announced that he was the Cazique, a kind of prince, of Poyais: a rich and healthy land on the coast of what is now Honduras, with a capital, a bank and British settlers already living there.

None of it was true. MacGregor printed Poyais banknotes, sold land and officers' posts, published a guidebook praising the country, and raised money in London with Poyais government bonds. Settlers swapped their real savings for his worthless Poyais dollars.

In 1822 and early 1823 two ships, the Honduras Packet and the Kennersley Castle, carried around 250 emigrants to Poyais. They found only jungle and swamp. Without shelter or supplies, many caught tropical diseases; about two thirds of them died. The survivors were rescued and taken to British Honduras (Belize), and only about 50 ever got back to Britain.

He got away with it

By the time the news reached London, MacGregor had moved to France and started the same scheme again. He was arrested and tried in Paris in 1826, but he was acquitted and only one of his associates was convicted. He kept trying smaller Poyais schemes in Britain for years.

In 1839 he moved to Venezuela, where he was given a general's pension for his service in the war of independence. He died in Caracas on 4 December 1845 and was buried with full military honours. He was never convicted for Poyais.
